Engineering Fundamentals: Rebar Weight & Takeoff

Reinforcement bar weight calculations form the foundation of structural material takeoffs, freight planning, and steel procurement. Structural carbon steel has a nominal density of 7,850 kg/m³. In metric jurisdictions governed by standards such as IS 456, BS 4449, or Eurocode 2, the unit weight per metre follows the direct mathematical relationship of bar diameter: kg/m = d² ÷ 162.28, where d is the nominal diameter in millimetres.

For US imperial reinforcement complying with ASTM A615 and ASTM A706, nominal bar designations (#3 through #18) are defined by eighths of an inch up to #8, transitioning to equivalent area cylinders for larger bars. Commercial suppliers bill against certified ASTM weight-per-foot specifications rather than pure volumetric cylinders to account for mill deformation ribs.

Weight — FAQ

How is the metric rebar weight formula d² / 162.28 derived?

Unit weight equals cross-sectional area multiplied by steel density: Area = (π/4)·d². Multiplying by density (7,850 kg/m³) and converting units gives (π/4)·(d/1000)² · 7850 · 1m = d² / 162.28 kg/m.

What is the weight of common metric rebar sizes?

8mm = 0.395 kg/m, 10mm = 0.617 kg/m, 12mm = 0.888 kg/m, 16mm = 1.578 kg/m, 20mm = 2.466 kg/m, 25mm = 3.853 kg/m, and 32mm = 6.313 kg/m.

How do I convert steel weight into purchase orders?

Sum your schedule's total weight in kilograms and convert to metric tons (divide by 1,000) or US short tons (divide by 907.2). Always add 3–5% for cutting offcuts and lap splices.
