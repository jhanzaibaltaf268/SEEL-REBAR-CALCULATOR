Rebar Weight Calculator

Find the weight of any reinforcement bar in seconds. Pick the bar size, enter the length and quantity, and get the total in kilograms, pounds and metric tons — using the industry-standard weight per metre = d² ÷ 162.28 formula.

How to use it

  1. Pick the bar size — metric (mm) or US (# size) — from the dropdown.
  2. For a known batch, leave the mode on Standard, enter the length of one bar in metres and the number of pieces.
  3. Read the total in kg, lb, metric tons and US short tons, plus the weight of one piece.
  4. For a known weight, switch the mode to Reverse, enter the total weight and your stock bar length to get total length and bars needed.
  5. Add waste, laps and hooks before ordering — the calculator weighs straight bars only.

Formula

Weight per metre (kg) = d² ÷ 162.28, where d is the bar diameter in mm. Total = weight/m × length × quantity.

Questions

What is the weight of a 12mm rebar?

12² ÷ 162.28 = 0.887 kg per metre on this calculator, so one 12 m bar weighs 10.64 kg and 100 of them weigh 1,064.4 kg. You will also see 0.888 kg/m quoted widely — that comes from the d²/162.2 shortcut (144 ÷ 162.2 = 0.888), and 0.889 kg/m from d²/162. The difference is about 0.2%: on a tonne order of 12 mm it is roughly 2 kg. Always check which constant a table uses before comparing totals.

How do you calculate the weight of steel rebar?

Square the diameter in mm, divide by 162.28 to get kg/m, then multiply by length and quantity. For 100 bars of 12 mm at 12 m: 0.887 × 12 × 100 = 1,064.4 kg. In pounds, multiply kg by 2.20462 (1,064.4 kg = 2,346.6 lb); in metric tons divide by 1000 (1.06 t). The formula section below derives the 162.28 constant from steel density.

What is the difference between d²/162 and d²/162.28?

162 is the rounded constant; 162.28 is the precise one. Steel at 7850 kg/m³ gives weight per metre = π/4 × d² × 7850 ÷ 1,000,000 = 0.0061654 × d², and 1 ÷ 0.0061654 = 162.2 — rounded to 162 for site arithmetic, kept as 162.28 by this calculator. On 12 mm bar that is 0.889 vs 0.887 kg/m, 10.67 vs 10.64 kg per 12 m bar, and about 2.1 kg on a tonne order. Neither is wrong; they are different roundings, and mixing them is how two estimates for the same steel disagree.

How many bars are in a tonne?

It depends on the bar size AND the bar length — a figure without a length is meaningless. For 12 m stock bars, one tonne needs: 8 mm 212 bars (211 full + 6.07 m offcut), 10 mm 136 (135 + 3.38 m), 12 mm 94 (93 + 11.4 m), 16 mm 53 (52 + 9.71 m), 20 mm 34 (33 + 9.68 m), 25 mm 22 (21 + 7.67 m), 32 mm 14 (13 + 2.48 m). For 40 ft (12.192 m) bars the counts are lower — 12 mm is 92, not 94 — because each bar is longer. The per-tonne table below gives the full reverse-mode figures.

What does a bundle of rebar weigh?

Bundle sizes vary by supplier, so weigh by count, not by the word bundle: 100 bars of 12 m weigh 266.40 kg (6 mm), 472.80 kg (8 mm), 739.20 kg (10 mm), 1,064.4 kg (12 mm), 1,893.6 kg (16 mm), 2,958 kg (20 mm), 4,621.2 kg (25 mm) and 7,572 kg (32 mm). One published supplier chart packs 10 × 8 mm, 7 × 10 mm, 5 × 12 mm, 3 × 16 mm, 2 × 20 mm and 1 × 25/32 mm per bundle — those piece counts are that supplier's, not a standard. Always confirm pieces per bundle on your delivery note.

How do I work backwards from a weight to bars?

Switch the calculator to Reverse mode, enter the weight and your stock length. For 2000 kg of 12 mm bar at 12 m stock it gives 2,254.8 m of bar (7,397.6 ft), 188 bars needed — 187 full bars plus a 10.79 m offcut. Note the ceil: ordering 188 full bars weighs 2,001.07 kg, slightly MORE than the 2000 kg you entered, because the offcut still has to be bought as a whole bar. If you order only the 187 full bars you will be 10.79 m short.

Does the bar grade change the weight?

No. Fe415, Fe500, Fe550 and Grade 60 differ in strength, not density, so the weight per metre is the same for a given diameter — and the same holds for TMT bars, which are heat-treated but still 7850 kg/m³ steel. Stainless reinforcement is the exception (about 7900–8000 kg/m³, roughly 1% heavier), and it is not what this calculator models. Grade affects laps, development and design — see the lap calculator and the development length calculator — not weight.

Why does delivered steel weigh differently from the table?

Because the table is theoretical (nominal diameter, exact density) and real bars vary within manufacturing tolerance: ASTM A615 allows ±6% on #3–#5 and ±4% on #6–#18, EN 10080/ISO 6935 allows ±6% up to 8 mm and ±4.5% above, and AS/NZS 4671 allows ±4% on the batch mean. On a 50 tonne order that compounds to tonnes, not kilograms. Check deliveries the way site engineers do: count the bars against the reverse-mode figure (3000 kg of 12 mm 12 m bars should be 282 bars — 281 full + 10.19 m), then weigh a sample (5 × 12 m 12 mm bars should be 53.22 kg). If the gap exceeds the applicable tolerance, raise it with the supplier — and check whether your order prices theoretical or actual (weighed) weight.

What is the difference between a metric ton and a US short ton?

A metric ton (tonne) is 1000 kg; a US short ton is 2000 lb = 907.18 kg — about 10% smaller. On the calculator defaults (100 × 12 mm × 12 m) that is 1.06 metric tons but 1.17 US short tons, for the same steel. Suppliers quoting tons without saying which one are quoting two different quantities: a price per short ton looks about 10% cheaper than the same price per metric ton. Convert any quote on the tonnage calculator before comparing.

Does the calculator include waste, laps, hooks or cost?

No — it weighs straight bars only. Add 5–10% for cutting and laps (published takeoff guides use 10% for laps and waste combined; one bar-schedule method uses 3–5% for cutting alone): the defaults total goes from 1,064.4 kg neat to 1,117.62 kg at +5% and 1,170.84 kg at +10%, which costs €1,277.28 / €1,341.14 / €1,405.01 at an example €1.20/kg.
