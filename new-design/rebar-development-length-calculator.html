Rebar Development Length Calculator

Compute the embedment a bar needs to develop its full strength. Select the bar size, steel grade and concrete grade, and the tool returns Ld in mm and as a multiple of bar diameter, for tension or compression.

How to use it

  1. Pick the bar size.
  2. Select the steel grade (Fe250–Fe550) and concrete grade (M20–M40).
  3. Choose tension or compression.
  4. Read Ld in mm, as a multiple of diameter, and in metres — plus the τbd used.
  5. Compare with the tables below before detailing the anchorage.

Formula

Ld = (φ × 0.87 × fy) ÷ (4 × τbd). τbd is the design bond stress (increased 60% for deformed bars, +25% for compression).

Questions

What is development length of rebar?

The length of bar that must be embedded in concrete to transfer its design force safely. Without enough embedment, the bar slips or pulls out before reaching its design stress.

What is the development length for Fe500 in M25?

About 47–49 times the bar diameter in tension for deformed bars — exactly 48.55 × d with this calculator: 582.59 mm for a 12 mm bar, 776.79 mm for a 16 mm bar, both in M25 tension.

Is development length different in tension and compression?

Yes. Compression raises the bond stress by 25%, so Ld is 80% of the tension value: a 12 mm Fe500 bar in M25 needs 582.59 mm in tension but 466.07 mm in compression.

Why is the stress 0.87 × fy and not fy?

0.87 is 1 ÷ 1.15, the partial safety factor for steel in IS 456. The design stress is the yield strength reduced by that factor — using full fy would overstate Ld by 15%.

Do deformed bars need shorter development length than plain bars?

Yes — deformed bars get a 60% higher bond stress (for example M25: 2.24 instead of 1.4 N/mm²), so their Ld is proportionally shorter. This calculator already uses deformed values.

Can a bend or hook replace straight development length?

Partly. A 90° bend counts as 8 × d and a U-hook as 16 × d toward Ld, with the rest made up in straight length. For cutting lengths, use the hook calculator.

What is the difference between development length and lap length?

Development length anchors one bar into concrete; lap length overlaps two bars to transfer force between them. A lap must be at least the development length, but lap rules add their own conditions — see the lap calculator.

Does stronger concrete reduce development length?

Yes. Higher grades raise the bond stress, so Ld falls: Fe500 in tension needs 56.64 × d in M20 but only 35.77 × d in M40.

Does the ×d multiple change with bar size?

No. For fixed steel and concrete grades the multiple is constant — only the length in mm grows with diameter. Fe500 in M25 is 48.55 × d whether the bar is 12 mm or 20 mm.

Does this calculator follow ACI or IS 456?

IS 456. The ACI method uses different factors (casting position, epoxy coating, bar size, concrete strength) and gives different lengths — do not mix the two methods on one detail.
