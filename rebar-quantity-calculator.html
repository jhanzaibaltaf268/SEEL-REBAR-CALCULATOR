How to use: Rebar Quantity & Takeoff Calculator

  1. Enter the area to be reinforced in square metres.
  2. Set the grid spacing in mm — the same spacing both ways.
  3. Pick the bar size for the kg/m² figure and the total.
  4. Read the total weight, the density in kg/m² and kg/ft², the total bar length and the tonnage.
  5. Add waste, laps and any second layer before ordering — the calculator gives the neat single-grid total only.

Formula

Bar length per m² = 2 ÷ spacing(m). Weight/m² = that × kg/m. Total = weight/m² × area.

The formula, explained

A grid has bars running both ways, so each square metre contains one metre of bar per direction for every metre of spacing — that is the whole formula:

Bar length per m² = 2 ÷ spacing (m); Weight per m² = that × kg/m; Total = weight per m² × area

At the defaults spacing of 200 mm, that is 2 ÷ 0.2 = 10.00 m of bar per m², and at 0.616 kg/m for 10 mm bars that is 6.16 kg/m². Multiply by the area and you have the total: 6.16 × 50 = 308.00 kg. The per-ft² row divides by 10.7639 (1 m² = 10.7639 ft²), so 6.16 ÷ 10.7639 = 0.572 kg/ft². Two display notes: both bar fields read "0.617|10", but the panel computes with the option value 0.616 kg/m, so every weight below uses 0.616 — the raw field string would give 308.50 kg, not the panel's 308.00 kg. And totals at or above 1000 show one decimal with separators, so the 100 m² example reads "1,182.7" kg, not 1182.67 kg — quote the panel form and your reader's check will match.

Worked example 1: defaults (50 m²)

Open the calculator and leave the defaults: area 50 m², spacing 200 mm, bars 10 mm.

Bar length per m² = 2 ÷ 0.2 = 10.00 m. Weight per m² = 10.00 × 0.616 = 6.16 kg/m². Per ft² = 6.16 ÷ 10.7639 = 0.572 kg/ft².

Total = 6.16 × 50 = 308.00 kg (0.308 t), from 500.00 m of bar. The panel shows 308.00 kg, 6.16 kg/m², 0.572 kg/ft², 500.00 m and 0.308 t. That is the neat single-grid total: no waste, no laps, no hooks, no second layer. The order section below turns it into something you can buy.

Worked example 2: 100 m² slab

Set area 100 m², spacing 150 mm, bars 12 mm.

Bar length per m² = 2 ÷ 0.15 = 13.33 m. Weight per m² = 13.33 × 0.887 = 11.83 kg/m². Per ft² = 11.83 ÷ 10.7639 = 1.10 kg/ft² (panel: 1.10).

Total = 11.83 × 100 = 1,182.7 kg (1.18 t), from 1,333.3 m of bar. Note the panel forms: the exact total is 1182.6667 kg, which displays as "1,182.7", and the exact length 1333.3333 m displays as "1,333.3" — totals at or above 1000 show one decimal, not two. Tightening the spacing from 200 mm to 150 mm and stepping up from 10 mm to 12 mm together nearly doubles the defaults density (6.16 → 11.83 kg/m²); spacing and bar size multiply, they do not add.

Worked example 3: small 20 m² area

Set area 20 m², spacing 300 mm, bars 8 mm.

Bar length per m² = 2 ÷ 0.3 = 6.67 m. Weight per m² = 6.67 × 0.394 = 2.63 kg/m². Per ft² = 2.63 ÷ 10.7639 = 0.244 kg/ft².

Total = 2.63 × 20 = 52.53 kg (0.053 t), from 133.33 m of bar. Small areas are where the density method is weakest: the formula assumes an endless grid, so on 20 m² the missing edge bars and the missing cover deduction are a larger share of the total than on 100 m². For anything this small that you actually intend to order, cross-check against the slab calculator with the real panel dimensions — the bridge section shows the size of the difference.

Against the exact slab count

This calculator and the slab calculator answer different questions, and on the same slab they disagree by a small, predictable amount. The slab tool counts real bars: it deducts 40 mm of cover from every bar length and counts the edge bars explicitly. This tool spreads 2 ÷ spacing metres over the area with no cover taken off. Three comparisons, all at 40 mm cover on the slab tool:

The density figure always runs high here, and the gap shrinks as the slab grows — the cover deduction is a fixed amount per bar, so it matters less on a big slab than a small one. Practical rule: use this page when you need a number in seconds (a quote, a sanity check, a big-area estimate); use the slab calculator when you need the bars to order. And note the direction: a density estimate that runs high is safe for budgeting and short for nothing — but it is not a bar list, and nobody can cut bars from a kg/m² figure.

From neat total to order

The panel total is neat grid steel. An order needs three additions, all worked here from the panel totals (exact totals throughout, not rounded display values).

Waste. Defaults neat 308.00 kg → 323.40 kg at +5%, 338.80 kg at +10%. House example (200 m², 200 mm, 12 mm): neat 1,774 kg → 1,862.70 kg at +5%, 1,951.40 kg at +10%.

Cost. At an example €1.20/kg (prices are examples — enter your local rate on the cost calculator): defaults €369.60 neat, €388.08 at +5%, €406.56 at +10%; house €2,128.80 neat, €2,235.24 at +5%, €2,341.68 at +10%.

Sticks. Total length ÷ stock length, rounded up: defaults 500.00 m → 42 × 12 m bars (41.67) or 84 × 6 m bars (83.33); house 2,000 m → 167 × 12 m bars (166.67). That is before cutting losses — bars cut to panel sizes leave offcuts, so treat the stick count as a floor. Laps sit on top of all three: wherever a run exceeds stock length, add the splice length per splice from the lap calculator.

For scale: 500 m² at 200 mm spacing with 12 mm bars is 4,435 kg (4.44 t), and 1000 m² at 150 mm with 16 mm bars is 21,040 kg (21.04 t) — both panel totals, both before waste. At that size the density method earns its keep: counting bars by hand across 1000 m² is where the errors live.

Imperial conversions

The calculator takes square metres and millimetres, so convert first: 1 m² = 10.7639 ft², 1 in = 25.4 mm, 1 kg = 2.20 lb, 1 kg/m² = 0.205 lb/ft².

Worked conversions: the defaults total 308.00 kg = 679.02 lb, and the defaults density 6.16 kg/m² = 1.26 lb/ft². A full imperial run — a 1000 ft² slab (92.90 m²), 12 in spacing both ways (304.8 mm), #4 verticals and horizontals (12.7 mm, 0.994 kg/m) — gives 6.52 kg/m², 0.606 kg/ft², 609.60 m of bar, 605.94 kg (0.606 t, 1,335.87 lb). With #5 bars (15.9 mm, 1.552 kg/m) at the same spacing the same slab needs 10.18 kg/m², 0.946 kg/ft², 609.60 m, 946.10 kg (0.946 t, 2,085.79 lb). The bar dropdowns include US sizes, so enter the metric equivalents of your dimensions and pick the matching US bar. Note the area conversion trap: 50 m² is 538.19 ft², not 500 ft² — a "square foot" takeoff that divides by 10 instead of 10.7639 undercounts the area by 7%.

What this calculator does not cover

  • Bar counts and per-bar lengths — it gives total metres and total weight, not a cutting list. For counts use the slab calculator or the member tools.
  • Laps, hooks, bends, cranks and development — see the lap calculator, the hook calculator and the development length calculator.
  • Second layers and double mats — the total is one grid; a two-layer slab needs the total doubled (plus chairs between layers).
  • Beams, columns, footings and walls — use the beam, column, footing and wall calculators; the concrete estimator covers the kg/m³-ratio method across a whole element.
  • Openings, steps, upstands, thickenings and edge beams — an area input cannot see any of them; deduct or add them by hand.
  • Chairs and spacers — see the chair calculator; tie wire is on the tie wire calculator.
  • Concrete volume — the concrete estimator gives volume plus steel at a kg/m³ ratio.
  • Waste and cost — the order section shows the arithmetic; price the final figure on the cost calculator and convert it on the tonnage calculator.
  • Bar area (Ast) and minimum-steel checks — the area calculator gives Ast and the steel percentage for a section.
  • Spacing checks against code maxima — the spacing calculator works bar counts from a length and a target spacing.

5 common mistakes

  1. Using one spacing for a two-spacing grid. The calculator takes a single spacing for both directions. If the drawing specifies 150 mm one way and 200 mm the other, this tool cannot model it — use the slab calculator, or the hand density 1 ÷ 0.15 + 1 ÷ 0.2 = 11.67 m/m² (7.19 kg/m² at 0.616 kg/m). Entering either spacing alone misstates the total by 14% on this example, and far more on wider splits.
  2. Ordering the neat total. The panel total has no waste, no laps and no hooks in it. On the defaults, ordering 308.00 kg flat leaves you short the moment the first bar is cut — 323.40 kg at +5% is the floor for an order, and laps sit on top.
  3. Using the wrong kg/m. The panel computes 10 mm bar at 0.616 kg/m (option value), not the 0.617 the field string reads, and several competitor pages quote the rounded d²/162 values (0.617/0.888) rather than this site's d²/162.28 options (0.616/0.887). The difference is ~1.6 kg per tonne on 10 mm bar — small per metre, visible per truckload, and the reason two sites' totals for the same grid never quite agree.
  4. Confusing kg/m² with kg/m³. A density of 6.16 kg/m² is not a small number for a slab — but a figure quoted as "80–120 kg per square metre" for slabs, as one method guide puts it, is a kg/m³-scale rate wearing the wrong unit. Convert with the thickness (kg/m³ = kg/m² ÷ thickness in m): 6.16 kg/m² on a 150 mm slab is 41.07 kg/m³, which is the right order of magnitude. If a quoted kg/m² figure looks ten times too big, it probably is.
  5. Trusting an area input to see shape. 50 m² can be a 10 × 5 m slab, a 25 × 2 m strip, or an L-shape with a stair opening — and the laps, edge bars and offcuts differ wildly between them while this calculator returns the same 308.00 kg for all three. Area is the fastest input and the blindest: the moment shape, openings or long runs matter, switch to the slab or member tool with real dimensions.

Rebar Quantity & Takeoff Calculator — FAQ

How much rebar per square foot?

It depends on spacing and bar size — that is exactly what the per-ft² row answers. On the calculator defaults (10 mm bars at 200 mm spacing) it is 0.572 kg/ft² (1.26 lb/ft²). Tighten to 150 mm and it rises to 0.763 kg/ft²; step up to 12 mm at 200 mm and it is 0.824 kg/ft². The spacing table below covers the common combinations.

How do you do a rebar takeoff?

Start with the density method this calculator uses: bar length per m² = 2 ÷ spacing in metres, times the bar's kg/m, times the area. That gives a fast neat total — 308.00 kg for the defaults (50 m², 200 mm, 10 mm). Then refine it: add 5–10% waste, add laps where bars run longer than stock, and for fabrication build a bar bending schedule — mark, diameter, shape, length, count and weight per bar — on the pattern the method guides describe. For exact panel counts use the slab calculator and the member tools.

What does this calculator assume?

A single grid, the same spacing both ways, one bar size, and an endless grid: 2 ÷ spacing metres of bar per m² across the whole area, with no cover deduction, no edge bars, no openings and no second layer. That is why it is a fast estimate and not a schedule — the slab-bridge section below shows it running 1–2% above the exact slab-tool count on small slabs.

Why does the slab calculator give a different total?

Because it counts real bars: it deducts cover from every bar length and counts the edge bars explicitly, while this calculator spreads 2 ÷ spacing metres over the area with no cover taken off. On a 5 × 4 m slab at 200 mm spacing with 10 mm bars, this calculator gives 123.20 kg and the slab tool gives 120.98 kg — 2.22 kg (1.83%) apart. On a 10 × 8 m slab the gap shrinks to 0.91%. Use this page for speed, the slab page for the order.

How much waste should I add?

Add 5–10% on top of the neat total for cutting and offcuts — published takeoff guides use 10% for lap splices and waste combined, and one bar-schedule method uses 3–5% for cutting losses alone. On the defaults that is 323.40 kg at +5% and 338.80 kg at +10%. Laps are separate: if a bar run exceeds the stock length, add the splice length per splice from the lap calculator — do not assume the waste percentage already covers it.

Does the calculator include laps or hooks?

No. The total is straight, neat grid steel only. Hooks, bends and anchorage add length per bar — work them out on the hook calculator and the development length calculator — and laps add a splice length wherever a run exceeds stock. A takeoff that ignores both under-orders on every long run at once.

What if my grid has different spacings each way?

This calculator takes one spacing for both directions, so it cannot model that grid directly. Two options: use the slab calculator, which takes the panel dimensions and counts both directions exactly; or work the density by hand as length per m² = 1 ÷ spacing-X + 1 ÷ spacing-Y — for 150 mm one way and 200 mm the other that is 6.67 + 5.00 = 11.67 m/m², which at 0.616 kg/m is 7.19 kg/m² (derived arithmetic, not a panel output).

How do I convert kg/m² to kg/m³ or lb/ft²?

Divide by the thickness for kg/m³: kg/m³ = kg/m² ÷ thickness in metres. The defaults density of 6.16 kg/m² on a 150 mm slab is 41.07 kg/m³. For lb/ft², multiply kg/m² by 0.205 (1 kg/m² = 0.205 lb/ft²): 6.16 kg/m² = 1.26 lb/ft², matching the panel's 0.572 kg/ft² row. Do not confuse the two: a figure quoted as kg/m² that looks like 80–120 is almost certainly a kg/m³ rate mislabelled — real slab densities run about 3–25 kg/m².

How many bars or sticks do I need to order?

Divide the total bar length by the stock length and round up: the defaults total 500.00 m needs 42 × 12 m bars (500 ÷ 12 = 41.67) or 84 × 6 m bars (500 ÷ 6 = 83.33). The house example below (2,000 m) needs 167 × 12 m bars. That is before cutting losses — bars cut to panel sizes leave offcuts, so the stick count from a length division is a floor, not a final order. Price the result on the cost calculator and convert it on the tonnage calculator.

Does it cover beams, columns, footings or walls?

No — this is an area-grid estimator only. It cannot see member geometry, so it cannot take off a beam cage, column verticals, footing mats or wall grids. Use the member tools — beam, column, footing, wall, pool — or the concrete estimator for a kg/m³-ratio estimate across a whole element. Chairs are on the chair calculator and tie wire on the tie wire calculator.
