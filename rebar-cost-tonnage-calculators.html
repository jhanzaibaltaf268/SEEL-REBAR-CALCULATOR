Commercial Procurement: Rebar Tonnage & Costing

Commercial rebar estimating bridges structural engineering drawings and steel mill procurement. Reinforcing steel is quoted and sold by weight—either per metric ton (1,000 kg / 2,204.6 lbs) in metric markets or per US short ton (2,000 lbs / 907.2 kg) or hundredweight (cwt) in US imperial markets.

Accurate purchasing requires accounting for standard mill stock lengths (typically 12 metres in metric jurisdictions, or 20ft / 40ft / 60ft in imperial markets). Because standard bars must be cut and spliced on site, a cutting scrap and wastage allowance (typically 3% to 5% for simple slabs, rising to 7% to 10% for complex frames) must be budgeted to prevent project shortages.

Commercial — FAQ

What wastage percentage should be allowed when ordering rebar?

Allow 3% to 5% for standard foundations, footings, and slabs. For complex structures with numerous bends, cutouts, and lap splices, budget 7% to 10% to prevent site shortages.

What is the difference between a metric ton and a US short ton?

A metric ton is exactly 1,000 kg (2,204.6 lbs). A US short ton is 2,000 lbs (907.185 kg). Always verify which ton unit your steel supplier's quote is based on.

How many 12-metre bars are in one metric ton?

For 12mm bar (0.888 kg/m), each 12m bar weighs ~10.66 kg, giving ~94 bars per ton. For 16mm bar (1.578 kg/m), each 12m bar weighs ~18.94 kg, giving ~53 bars per ton.
