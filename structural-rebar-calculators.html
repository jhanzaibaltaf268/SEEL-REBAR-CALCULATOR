Reinforced Concrete Member Design Principles

Structural members must resist complex combinations of axial gravity loads, bending moments, and lateral shear forces. Under IS 456, ACI 318, and Eurocode 2, member detailing separates steel into two vital functions: primary longitudinal reinforcement to resist tension and compression, and transverse confinement steel (stirrups and column ties) to prevent diagonal shear failure and bar buckling.

In columns, vertical bars are confined within closed lateral ties that maintain column core integrity under extreme seismic and axial loads. In beams, bottom tensile bars carry positive sagging moments, while top bars carry negative hogging moments over supports. Our member calculators compute both longitudinal and transverse steel simultaneously.

Members — FAQ

What is the minimum number of vertical bars in a column?

Rectangular columns require a minimum of 4 longitudinal bars (one in each corner); circular columns require at least 6 bars. Minimum bar diameter is generally 12mm (#4).

Why are beam stirrups spaced closer near support faces?

Shear forces peak at beam supports and decrease towards midspan. Tighter stirrup spacing (100–150mm) near supports prevents 45-degree diagonal shear cracks.

How are wall vertical bars anchored into footings?

Vertical bars in retaining walls must be lapped with starter dowels anchored into the footing pad by at least the full development length (Ld) to transfer bending moment.
