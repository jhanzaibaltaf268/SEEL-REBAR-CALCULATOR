Concrete Reinforcement Estimating Best Practices

Estimating rebar for cast-in-place concrete requires balancing three governing factors: structural grid spacing, clear cover offsets to prevent environmental corrosion, and lap splices required when spans exceed standard stock lengths (6m, 9m, or 12m). While rough preliminary budgets often assume rule-of-thumb ratios (80–120 kg of steel per m³ of slab concrete), actual construction ordering demands precise piece takeoffs.

In two-way slabs, foundation mats, and footings, rebar is placed in orthogonal layers (bottom tension mats and suspended top mats). Our estimating tools automatically deduct edge cover on both sides before dividing by design spacing, giving you the exact piece counts and total bar lengths needed for orders.

Estimating — FAQ

What rebar size and spacing is typical for a concrete slab?

Residential ground slabs commonly specify #3 (10mm) or #4 (12mm) bars at 150mm to 300mm (6" to 12") centres both ways, placed on bar chairs to maintain cover.

What is clear cover and why is it deducted from slab dimensions?

Clear cover is the distance between outer rebar and the concrete face. It protects steel against moisture and fire. Subtracting double the edge cover ensures bars fit within formwork.

How much extra rebar should be ordered for lap splices?

Whenever a slab dimension exceeds standard bar length (typically 12m or 40ft), bars must overlap. Plan an additional 5% to 10% steel allowance for splices and cutting scrap.
